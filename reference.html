Badge

A small status marker: a coloured dot on its own, or an outlined label. The colour sits in the dot and the outline, never in the text.

Partsrootlabeloutlineiconicons-dot

When to use

  • Marking the status of something in a list, a card or a table row: Paid, Overdue, Draft.
  • Small when there's no room for words, or the status is obvious from where it sits. Just the coloured dot.

When not to use

  • Telling people something about the whole page. That's InlineBanner.
  • Describing what something is about rather than its status. That's Tag.
  • Anything clickable. Badge doesn't do hover or focus, Chip does.

Behaviour

Two sizes now. Small is only the dot, 24 by 24, coloured by status. Medium is the label with an outline, and status changes only the outline (non-interactive/background/<status>/light). The text stays non-interactive/foreground/tertiary whatever the status, on purpose: I keep badges quiet. There's no background fill either. My first Badge in akount.ing got exactly that wrong. The dot in front of the label is optional now (showIcon), and off unless you ask for it.

Accessibility

  • Element: a <span>. Nothing to click.
  • Colour: the colour alone isn't enough. On Medium the text says the status. On Small there's no text, so give it an aria-label ("Overdue") or put the status in words right next to it.
  • Dot: aria-hidden="true" when there's a label next to it.

Content

One or two words. Sentence case, no punctuation.

Button

A 48px button with just a label, in four levels of emphasis.

Figma 39:38 contract: review guidance: agreed not built yet 20 variants
typeBorderlessPrimarySecondaryTertiary
statesDefaultHoverPressedFocusDisabled
Partsrootlabelbackgroundtextfocus-ring

When to use

  • Doing something: save, send, continue, open.
  • Primary for the one thing a screen is really about. One per screen, no more.
  • Secondary for the options next to it, Tertiary when little is at stake, Borderless in busy places where an outline would just be noise.

When not to use

  • Actions that need an icon to make sense: use PillButton. Icon only: IconButton.
  • Going somewhere. That's a link. A button that navigates breaks open-in-new-tab and the back button.
  • Picking between options. That's Toggle or Chip.

Behaviour

type sets how loud it is. state is never something you pass in code: hover, pressed and focus come from :hover, :active and :focus-visible. Disabled is the only one you set yourself. The background fades to opacity/gentle and the label stays readable.

Accessibility

  • Element: <button type="button">, or type="submit" inside a form.
  • Name: the label people see. Don't swap it for an aria-label that says something else.
  • Keyboard: Enter and Space.
  • Focus: the focus-ring part, 2px, only on :focus-visible.
  • Disabled: I'd rather use aria-disabled="true" with a short reason nearby than disabled. disabled drops the button out of the tab order and nobody can find out why it doesn't work.

Content

A verb, sentence case, two or three words. "Save changes", not "OK" or "SUBMIT". Say what will happen.

A filled status pill with an icon and a label, in four statuses and two sizes.

When to use

  • Telling people something about the page or a section they should notice: saved, offline, open for work, needs your attention.
  • Small in a line of text, Medium on its own line or at the top of a section.

When not to use

  • The status of one item in a list or table. That's Badge, smaller and quieter.
  • Anything that needs a decision. That's a dialog. A banner tells, it doesn't ask.

Behaviour

status (Default, Positive, Negative, Warning) sets the fill and the icon: plus, check, alarm, warning. You can't interact with it: no hover, no focus.

Accessibility

  • Element: a <span> or a <p>. If it shows up because of something the person just did, put it in a live region (role="status", or role="alert" for Negative).
  • Colour: the icon carries the status as well as the fill. Until the icons are exported (akount.ing has a deviation for this), the words have to say the status.
  • Glyph: aria-hidden="true". The label carries the meaning.

Content

One short sentence, no full stop if it's a fragment. Lead with what happened: "Saved just now", "Payment failed: card expired".

Where code differs on purpose

  • akount.ing: Renders label-only; the per-status glyph (plus / alarm / positive / warning) is an optional slot. The kit glyphs are not exported yet, and drawing substitutes would ship something the kit does not contain.

When to use

  • Any single line of text: email, name, an amount, search.

When not to use

  • Picking from a known list. That's Chip or Toggle, free text just invites typos.
  • Several lines of text. There's no textarea in the kit yet.

Behaviour

Four things change it:

  • configuration: Empty shows the label where you'll type. Filled moves it up and shows the value.
  • selected: it has focus, so the focus ring shows.
  • error: the outline, label, help text and icons go red, and the alarm icon shows up on the right.
  • state: Default, Hover and Disabled.

Check what people typed when they leave the field or submit, not on every key.

Accessibility

  • Element: a real <input> with a <label for>. The floating label is the label. Never a placeholder.
  • Supportive Text: connected with aria-describedby.
  • Error: aria-invalid="true", and the help text says how to fix it.
  • Colour: an error shows as the alarm icon and the message, not only red.
  • Focus: the ring shows on :focus-visible and when you arrive by keyboard. Never remove the outline without the ring.
  • Lock Icon: just decoration, unless it means read-only. If it does, set readonly and say so in the help text.

Content

Label: a noun, sentence case ("Email address"). Help text: one line. Before an error it's a hint, after it it's the fix ("Use at least 8 characters", not "Invalid input").

Paper effect

The sheet of dots behind runceanu.com's hero. It reacts to the cursor, the buttons and clicks, and never moves by itself.

runceanu-portfolio/src/components/HeroBackdrop.jsx contract: review guidance: agreed runceanu.com
Partssheetdotlensripplepen
sheet
A canvas of dots behind the hero. A CSS mask shapes it: on runceanu.com that's a 1104px circle centred on the hero text (a 552px radial gradient, solid to 40%, gone by 92%). It's sized so the dot grid lines up with the content, not the container. When nothing's happening it costs one still frame, and with reduced motion you get the still sheet and none of what's below.
dot
One point on the grid. It never moves by itself, it only reacts (to the lens, the ripple, the pen). The paper should never twinkle.
lens
The dome every behaviour shares. It has three sources: the cursor (the magnifier), the button halo (hover a hero button and a lens rises under its centre, as if the button presses into the paper), and press ink (press down and the sheet dents, fast going in and slow coming back). Each dot looks at all three and the strongest wins, so lenses never add up. Dots inside grow and get pushed outward. They never multiply and never change shape.
ripple
A ring (a soft band, about 1.8s) that crosses the sheet from the middle. It's triggered by the availability dot's own pulse, every second beat: so every 8s, in step with it, never on a timer of its own. It only runs while the hero is on screen.
pen
Every 7 to 8 seconds the pen sketches one kit component in 2px lines (colour `non-interactive/foreground/secondary` at the ink opacity). It draws line by line (about 1.6s), holds (about 3s), then fades. The rule of the grid: outer shapes follow the dot rows and columns exactly, start on a dot, measure in whole cells, and round their corners between dots. Small inner details (a check, an x, a knob, bars, a caret) keep their natural shape. Every point of a line moves with the lens, so the ink bulges like the dots do. One sketch at a time.

When to use

  • Behind the home hero, and only there. It's the site's signature, and using it twice waters it down.

When not to use

  • Behind dense content or forms. At small sizes the dots fight with the text.

Behaviour

The dots only ever react, through the lens, the ripple and the pen described in the parts. They never animate on their own. Everything snaps to the 16px grid. When nothing happens it's one still frame, and it only runs while the hero is on screen.

Accessibility

  • Decorative: the canvas is aria-hidden="true" and never takes focus.
  • Motion: with prefers-reduced-motion you get the still sheet, and none of the movement.

When to use

  • Switching a view or a filter on and off, when the change happens straight away.
  • Picking one option from a short set, as a group. The year switcher in akount.ing works like this.

When not to use

  • Doing something. That's IconButton. A toggle remembers, a button doesn't.
  • A settings-style switch with a label. There isn't one in the kit yet, so ask for one rather than stretching Toggle.

Behaviour

It changes with selected and state. Off, it's a plate with 12px corners on the secondary surface, faded down. On, it becomes a fully round primary pill. The change of shape is part of the signal, not just the colour.

Accessibility

  • Alone: <button aria-pressed="true|false">.
  • Group: role="radio" with aria-checked, inside a role="radiogroup" that has a name. Arrow keys move between options, Tab gets you in and out.
  • Name: aria-label when it's icon only.
  • Focus: focus-ring (2px, actions/primary/focus/focus-ring when it's on), on :focus-visible.
  • Colour: on and off show in the shape (12px corners to fully round) as well as the colour.

Content

Icon only in the kit. akount.ing adds a text label, and that's a declared deviation. If you add one, keep it to a short word or a number.

Where code differs on purpose

  • akount.ing: Accepts a text label; width grows past 40px to fit it, height stays 40px. Label colour follows the surface: secondary label unselected, primary label selected. The year switcher needs text. The kit's toggle is icon-only and specifies no label colour.
  • akount.ing: Rendered as role=radio inside a ToggleGroup radio group, which has no surface of its own. Toggles are used as a single-choice set; ToggleGroup is not a kit component.
